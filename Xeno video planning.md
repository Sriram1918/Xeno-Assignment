# Xeno Video — Shooting Script (6:00, twelve 30-sec beats)

> **Format:** Shoot each beat as its own take. Stitch in edit. Never attempt 6 min in one pass.
> **On-camera:** Face-cam picture-in-picture. Full-frame face on the talking beats (1, 2, 7, 9, 11, 12). Small corner face on the live-demo beats (3, 4, 5, 6, 8, 10).
> **`[brackets]`** = fill in your real specifics (numbers, file names, the actual on-screen moment).

---

## Delivery rules (read once, internalize, then forget)

**The one mindset:** You are showing a clever thing to *one* friend who is smart but busy. Not "presenting." Not "pitching." Showing.

- **Pace:** Slightly slower than feels natural. When nervous, everyone speeds up. Force a half-beat pause after each sentence. Pauses read as confidence, not gaps.
- **Chill = breath + baseline smile.** Inhale before each take. Hold a soft, relaxed half-smile as your *resting* face. Brow unfurrowed. That alone reads as "this guy's got it handled."
- **Don't memorize word-for-word.** Memorize the *one point* of each beat (written at the top of each). Say it in your own words. A slightly loose natural line beats a perfect robotic one every time.
- **Eyes on the lens**, not your screen preview, during talking beats. Tape a tiny arrow next to the lens if it helps.
- **Hands in frame, calm.** Gesture on emphasis, rest between. No pen-clicking, no face-touching, no chair-swiveling.
- **Energy curve:** Start warm and grounded → build → peak at beat 7 (the proof) → land calm and certain at beat 12. Don't start at 100%; you'll have nowhere to go.

**Pre-shoot checklist (do not skip):**
- [ ] Demo seeded with clean data; the exact run you'll show is rehearsed and known-good 3+ times
- [ ] Reset/seed button tested (so you can re-shoot the demo identically)
- [ ] Screen font size bumped — code and dashboard readable at 1080p
- [ ] Notifications OFF (Do Not Disturb, close Slack/mail/phone)
- [ ] Mic test done; quiet room; no fan/echo
- [ ] Light source in front of your face (window or lamp), not behind
- [ ] The recovered-revenue number on screen is clean and believable

## BEAT 1 — Cold Open / Hook (0:00–0:30)
**One point:** Open on the payoff, not on "hi I'm." Earn attention in 5 seconds.

**On screen:** Full-frame face. (Optional: dashboard with the recovered-revenue number blurred/behind you.)

**Say (your words, this shape):**
> "Taco Bell is a real Xeno customer. So I asked a simple question — when a regular stops showing up, can an AI win them back, *and prove it actually caused the sale?* That's the whole product. A growth marketer types one sentence, and an agent finds the lapsed customers, writes the messages, sends them, and reports back the revenue it genuinely recovered. Let me show you."

**Tone:** Calm, a little playful on "real Xeno customer." Confident, unhurried.
**Face/body:** Open. Slight smile. Small lean-in on "*and prove it actually caused the sale.*" Eyebrows lift a touch on "one sentence."
**Retake if:** You said "um/so" at the start · you rushed the first line · you smiled nervously instead of warmly · energy was flat.

## BEAT 2 — The Bet & The Cut List (0:30–1:00)
**One point:** Show product judgment. What you built deeply, and what you deliberately refused to build.

**On screen:** Full-frame face. (Optional small lower-third text: "One job: win-back.")

**Say:**
> "The brief was wide open — ingest, segment, send, report. I could've built all of it, shallow. I didn't. I went deep on *one* job: winning back lapsed shoppers. On purpose, I did **not** build a manual segment builder — the agent is the segment builder. No login, no scheduling, no template library, no five use-cases. One job, done properly, beats five done halfway."

**Tone:** Matter-of-fact, slightly opinionated. This is the "I have taste" beat — own it.
**Face/body:** Steady. A small head-shake on "I didn't." Count off cuts lightly with fingers if natural, but don't over-do it.
**Retake if:** You sounded apologetic about the cuts (they're a strength — say them with a straight spine) · you listed cuts in a monotone · you hedged ("I think maybe").

## BEAT 3 — Demo: Goal → Audience (1:00–1:30)
**One point:** Plain English in → a *typed, real* audience out, in Xeno's own language.

**On screen:** Chat panel. You type the goal. Agent returns the segment. Face in corner.

**Do + say (narrate as it happens):**
> "Here's the only input." *(type:)* "*Win back customers who used to order weekly but haven't in a month.*" *(agent runs)* "It didn't guess — it turned that into a real query and pulled a real audience. [N] customers. Notice it's speaking like a marketer: it's calling these the *lapsed loyalists* persona — average order value [₹X], last visit [34] days out."

**Tone:** Tour-guide calm. Let the product talk; you're pointing.
**Face/body:** Look at the screen action, then flick eyes to camera on the punchline ("speaking like a marketer"). Small nod when the audience appears.
**Retake if:** The agent run lagged/errored (re-seed, re-shoot) · you talked over the key result instead of letting it land · numbers on screen were unreadable · you said "I think it found…" (you should *know*).

## BEAT 5 — Demo: The Human Gate / The Catch (2:00–2:30)
**One point:** The human-in-the-loop is real, not decorative — show it doing its job.

**On screen:** The approval screen. You review, and either edit one thing or approve. Face in corner.

**Say:**

**Tone:** Trustworthy, grounded. This beat builds *safety* credibility.
**Face/body:** Slightly more serious — eyebrows neutral, focused. Small confident nod on "Then I approve."
**Retake if:** You couldn't show a real "catch" (even a tiny edit is fine — but show *something* being human-decided) · the approve action lagged · you sounded unsure whether the gate mattered.

## BEAT 6 — Demo: Send → Async Receipts → Live Dashboard (2:30–3:00)
**One point:** The two-service callback loop is alive — receipts stream back and update state in real time.

**On screen:** Hit send → channel service simulates → receipts (delivered/opened/clicked) roll in → dashboard counters move. Face in corner.

**Say:**

**Tone:** A little build of energy here — things are moving on screen.
**Face/body:** Eyes track the updating numbers, then to camera for the tease ("we'll get to why it's tricky"). Slight smile of anticipation.
**Retake if:** Receipts didn't visibly update on screen · the loop stalled · you narrated faster than the screen could show it · dashboard numbers unreadable.

## BEAT 7 — THE PROOF: Recovered Revenue + Control Group (3:00–3:30)  ⭐ PEAK
**One point:** This is the moment. You answer the one question every CRM buyer asks: *how do you know the message caused the sale?*

**On screen:** The recovered-revenue number, big. Then reveal the control-group comparison (messaged vs holdout buy-rate). Full-frame face for the reveal, or split.

**Say:**
> "Here's the number it reports — [₹X] in recovered revenue. But here's the part I care about most. How do I know the *message* caused those orders, and not luck? Because the agent quietly held back a random ten percent and sent them *nothing*. A control group. The recovered revenue isn't everyone who bought — it's the *lift* over the people we stayed silent on. That's measured, not claimed."

**Tone:** This is your peak. Slow down. Let "*a control group*" land with a beat of silence after it. Quiet confidence beats loud hype.
**Face/body:** Lean in slightly. Eyebrows up on the number. Then settle, steady eye contact on "measured, not claimed." A small, certain nod to close.
**Retake if:** You rushed this (it's THE beat — give it room) · you said the number nervously · you didn't pause after "control group" · the lift logic came out muddled. **This beat gets the most takes. Earn it.**

## BEAT 8 — Architecture: Two Services + The Callback Loop (3:30–4:00)
**One point:** Clear diagram, clear reasoning. Why two services, why a callback loop.

**On screen:** Your architecture diagram. Face in corner. Trace the flow with cursor.

**Say:**
> "Architecture. Two services on purpose. The CRM owns customers, the agent, and the truth of every campaign. The channel service is separate — it's the stub, and keeping it separate mirrors reality, where the channel is a system you don't control. CRM calls channel to send; channel calls *back* with receipts. Two systems, talking asynchronously. That separation is the whole design."

**Tone:** Architect-calm. You're explaining a decision, not reading a diagram.
**Face/body:** Follow the cursor with your eyes; glance to camera on "on purpose" and "the whole design." Relaxed.
**Retake if:** Diagram was cluttered/unreadable · you described boxes instead of *reasoning* · cursor wandered aimlessly.

## BEAT 9 — The Async Loop & Scale Tradeoffs (4:00–4:30)
**One point:** The SDE flex. Name the hard parts and your conscious tradeoff.

**On screen:** Zoom into the loop part of the diagram (outbox → worker → receipts). Full-frame face works too. 

**Say:**
> "The hard part isn't sending — it's the receipts coming back at volume, out of order, sometimes twice, sometimes failing. So sends go through a Postgres-backed outbox with a background worker — retries built in, and every receipt is idempotent, so a duplicate or an out-of-order 'opened' can't corrupt the state. I deliberately skipped Redis and Celery for this scale — less to operate. At real volume I'd move to a broker like Kafka. I know exactly where my design stops."

**Tone:** This is where you sound like an engineer who's *thought about failure.* Confident, specific.
**Face/body:** Steady, a touch more intensity. Small nod on "I know exactly where my design stops" — that line is gold, deliver it clean.
**Retake if:** You hand-waved "out of order / idempotent" without conviction · you forgot to state the tradeoff *and* the at-scale answer · you sounded like you were reciting buzzwords instead of owning them.

**One point:** How you *directed and reviewed* AI to build this — honestly, not "I used AI."

**On screen:** Full-frame face. (Optional: a quick flash of a prompt/diff you actually used.)

**Tone:** Honest, mature. Not bragging about AI, not hiding it. You *led*, AI *executed*.
**Face/body:** Direct eye contact — this is a "who I am as an engineer" beat. Calm certainty.
**Retake if:** You made it sound like AI built it for you · OR like you barely used AI (both read wrong) · you got vague — give one *concrete* example of directing/cutting it back.

## Editing notes
- Trim dead air between beats but keep your natural half-beat pauses *inside* beats — they're the confidence.
- Add a 1-line on-screen caption for each section change (Product / Demo / Architecture / Code / AI Workflow) so a tired reviewer always knows where they are.
- Keep total under 6:10. If over, the first thing to tighten is beat 8 or 10, never beat 7.
- Watch the whole thing once at 1x with the sound OFF — your face alone should read calm and confident. If any beat looks tense on mute, re-shoot it.
